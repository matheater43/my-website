Question 2

Answer: D 81% Correct
\begin{align*} \frac{y-1}{c}&=\frac{y+1}{d}\\[1mm] d(y-1)&=c(y+1)\\ dy-d&=cy+c\\ dy-cy&=c+d\\ y(d-c)&=c+d\\ y&=\frac{c+d}{d-c} \end{align*}

Alternative Solution

Let $c=1$ and $d=2$.
For option A:
$y=\dfrac{c-d}{c+d}=\dfrac{1-2}{1+2}=-\dfrac{1}{3}$
$\dfrac{y-1}{c}=\dfrac{-\dfrac{1}{3}-1}{1}=-\dfrac{4}{3}$
$\dfrac{y+1}{d}=\dfrac{-\dfrac{1}{3}+1}{2}=\dfrac{1}{3}$
$\thus \dfrac{y-1}{c}\ne \dfrac{y+1}{d}$
For option B:
$y=\dfrac{d-c}{c+d}=\dfrac{2-1}{1+2}=\dfrac{1}{3}$
$\dfrac{y-1}{c}=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{3}-1}{1}=-\dfrac{2}{3}$
$\dfrac{y+1}{d}=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{3}+1}{2}=\dfrac{2}{3}$
$\thus \dfrac{y-1}{c}\ne \dfrac{y+1}{d}$
For option C:
$y=\dfrac{c+d}{c-d}=\dfrac{1+2}{1-2}=-3$
$\dfrac{y-1}{c}=\dfrac{-3-1}{1}=-4$
$\dfrac{y+1}{d}=\dfrac{-3+1}{2}=-1$
$\thus \dfrac{y-1}{c}\ne \dfrac{y+1}{d}$
(At this point, we know that the answer is D. Below is for checking only.)
For option D:
$y=\dfrac{c+d}{d-c}=\dfrac{1+2}{2-1}=3$
$\dfrac{y-1}{c}=\dfrac{3-1}{1}=2$
$\dfrac{y+1}{d}=\dfrac{3+1}{2}=2$
$\thus \dfrac{y-1}{c}= \dfrac{y+1}{d}$
$\thus$ The answer is D.

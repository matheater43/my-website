Program Logic

Let the objective function be $\mathrm f(x,y)= Ax + By$.
Suppose we want to find the minimum value of $\mathrm f$ subject to certain linear constraints.
Pick any two of the constraints.
By multiplying both sides by $-1$ if necessary, the two constraints can be written in the form
(*): $\begin{cases} Cx+Dy\ge k_1 &\text{ ......(1)}\\[1mm] Xx+Yy\ge k_2 &\text{ ......(2)} \end{cases}$
Let $P$ be the point of intersection of the straight lines $L_1: Cx + Dy = k_1$ and $L_2 : Xx + Yy = k_2$.
Suppose $P_1$ and $P_2$ are points other than $P$ satisfying (*), and they lie on $L_1$ and $L_2$ respectively.
Note that if the value of $\mathrm f$ decreases from $P_1$ to $P$ along $L_1$ and increases from $P$ to $P_2$ along $L_2$, then $P$ is a local minimum of $\mathrm f$.
Hence, we can take $k_1=k_2=0$ and (*) reduces to
(**): $\begin{cases} Cx+Dy\ge 0 &\text{ ......(3)}\\[1mm] Xx+Yy\ge 0 &\text{ ......(4)} \end{cases}$
Now $P=(0, 0)$ and $\mathrm f(0,0)=0$.
If the value of $\mathrm f$ decreases from $P_1$ to $(0, 0)$ along $L_1$ and increases from $(0, 0)$ to $P_2$ along $L_2$, then $(0, 0)$ is a local minimum of $\mathrm f$.
Take $P_1 = (D, -C)$. To check that $P_1$ satisfies (**), we require that $P_1$ satisfies (4), i.e.
$X(D) + Y(-C) > 0 \Leftrightarrow XD > YC$ ......(i)
Take $P_2 = (Y, -X)$. To check that $P_2$ satisfies (**), we require that $P_2$ satisfies (3), i.e.
$C(Y) + D(-X) > 0 \Leftrightarrow CY > DX$ ......(ii)
There are 4 cases.
Case 1: Both (i) and (ii) hold, i.e. $XD > YC$ and $CY > DX$.
$(0, 0)$ is a local minimum of $\mathrm f$ when $P$ decreases from $P_1 = (D, -C)$ to $(0, 0)$ along $L_1$ and increases from $(0, 0)$ to $P_2 = (Y, -X)$ along $L_2$, i.e.
\begin{alignat*}{2} A(D)+B(-C)&>0 \quad &&\text{and}\quad & 0&< A(Y)+B(-X)\\[1mm] AD&>BC \quad &&\text{and}\quad & AY&>BX \end{alignat*}
Case 2: (i) holds but (ii) does not hold, i.e. $XD > YC$ and $CY < DX$.
Then $P_2=(-Y,X)$ satifies (3).
$(0, 0)$ is a local minimum of $\mathrm f$ when $P$ decreases from $P_1 = (D, -C)$ to $(0, 0)$ along $L_1$ and increases from $(0, 0)$ to $P_2 = (-Y, X)$ along $L_2$, i.e.
\begin{alignat*}{2} A(D)+B(-C)&>0 \quad &&\text{and}\quad & 0&< A(-Y)+B(X)\\[1mm] AD&>BC \quad &&\text{and}\quad & AY&< BX \end{alignat*}
Case 3: (i) does not hold but (ii) holds, i.e. $XD < YC$ and $CY > DX$.
Then $P_1=(-D,C)$ satifies (4).
$(0, 0)$ is a local minimum of $\mathrm f$ when $P$ decreases from $P_1 = (-D, C)$ to $(0, 0)$ along $L_1$ and increases from $(0, 0)$ to $P_2 = (Y, -X)$ along $L_2$, i.e.
\begin{alignat*}{2} A(-D)+B(C)&>0 \quad &&\text{and}\quad & 0&< A(Y)+B(-X)\\[1mm] AD&< BC \quad &&\text{and}\quad & AY&>BX \end{alignat*}
Case 4: Both (i) and (ii) do not hold, i.e. $XD < YC$ and $CY < DX$.
Then $P_1=(-D,C)$ satifies (4) and $P_2=(-Y,X)$ satifies (3).
$(0, 0)$ is a local minimum of $\mathrm f$ when $P$ decreases from $P_1 = (-D, C)$ to $(0, 0)$ along $L_1$ and increases from $(0, 0)$ to $P_2 = (-Y, X)$ along $L_2$, i.e. \begin{alignat*}{2} A(-D)+B(C)&>0 \quad &&\text{and}\quad & 0&< A(-Y)+B(X)\\[1mm] AD&< BC \quad &&\text{and}\quad & AY&< BX \end{alignat*}
Combining the 4 cases, we know that $(0, 0)$ is a local minimum of $\mathrm f$ when $MAD > MBC$ and $NAY > NBX$, where
$(M, N) = (1, 1)$ in case 1;
$(M, N) = (1, -1)$ in case 2;
$(M, N) = (-1, 1)$ in case 3;
$(M, N) = (-1, -1)$ in case 4.
After running through all pairs of constraints, the minimum value of $\mathrm f$ is the maximum of the local minima.
If we want to find the maximum value of $P$ instead, then simply write each inequality with ‘$\le$’ instead and go through the same steps as above.
The feasible region of any pairs of constraints will switch to the opposite side of the point of intersection.
As a result, if originally $\mathrm f$ decreases from $P_1$ to $P$ along $L_1$ and increases from $P$ to $P_2$ along $L_2$, then now f increases from $P_1$ to $P$ along $L_1$ and decreases from $P$ to $P_2$ along $L_2$.
So $P$ is a maximum point of $\mathrm f$.
Note: Regardless of the number of constraints, all pairs of constraints must be tested in order to determine the maximum/minimum.

Question 6

Answer: A
$\begin{aligned} (x-a)(x-a-1)&=(x-a)\\ (x-a)(x-a-1)-(x-a)&=0\\ (x-a)[(x-a-1)-1]&=0\\ (x-a)(x-a-2)&=0\\ x&=a\quad\text{or}\quad x=a+2 \end{aligned}$

Alternative Solution

When $x=a$,
L.H.S.$\begin{aligned}[t] &=(a-a)(a-a-1)\\ &=0 \end{aligned}$
R.H.S.$\begin{aligned}[t] &=(a-a)\\ &=0 \end{aligned}$
$\bcz$ L.H.S. = R.H.S.
$\thus$ $x=a$ is a solution of the equation.
$\thus$ The answer is C or D.
For option C:
When $x=a+1$,
L.H.S.$\begin{aligned}[t] &=(a+1-a)(a+1-a-1)\\ &=0 \end{aligned}$
R.H.S.$\begin{aligned}[t] &=(a+1-a)\\ &=1 \end{aligned}$
$\bcz$ L.H.S. $\ne$ R.H.S.
$\thus$ $x=a+1$ is not a solution of the equation.
$\thus$ The answer is D.

Answer: D
$\begin{aligned} x^2-6x&=2-k\\ x^2-6x+k-2&=0 \end{aligned}$
$\bcz$ The equation has no real roots.
$\thus$$\begin{aligned}[t] \Delta&<0\\ (-6)^2-4(1)(k-2)&<0\\ 36-4k+8&<0\\ -4k&<-44\\ k&>11 \end{aligned}$

Alternative Solution 1

$\begin{aligned} x^2-6x&=2-k\\ x^2-6x+k-2&=0 \end{aligned}$
Note that the roots of the equation are the $x$-intercepts of the graph of the function $y=x^2-6x+k-2$. Since the graph opens upwards and its $y$-intercept is $k-2$, the graph has no $x$-intercepts when $k$ is larger than some positive value.
$\thus$ The answer is D.

Alternative Solution 2

$\begin{aligned} x^2-6x&=2-k\\ x^2-6x+k-2&=0 \end{aligned}$
Let $k=0$. The equation becomes $x^2-6x-2=0$. Using FMLA 01, the equation has real roots.
$\thus$ The required range of values of $k$ does not include $0$.
$\thus$ The answer is A or D.
Let $k=-8$. The equation becomes $x^2-6x+(-8)-2=0$. Using FMLA 01, the equation has real roots.
$\thus$ The required range of values of $k$ does not include $-8$.
$\thus$ The answer is D.

Let $z=kx^3y^2$, where $k$ is a non-zero constant.
Substitute $x=2$, $y=1$ and $z=14$ into the equation:
\begin{aligned} 14&=k(2)^3(1)^2\\[1mm] k&=\frac{7}{4} \end{aligned}

Alternative Solution

Let $z=kx^3y^2$, where $k$ is a non-zero constant.
$\thus\dfrac{z}{x^3y^2}=k$
For any two sets of values of $x$, $y$ and $z$, say $x_1$, $y_1$, $z_1$ and $x_2$, $y_2$, $z_2$:
$\dfrac{z_1}{x_1^3y_1^2}=\dfrac{z_2}{x_2^3y_2^2}$
Substitute $x_1=2$, $y_1=1$, $z_1=14$, $x_2=3$ and $y_2=-2$ into the equation: \begin{align*} \frac{14}{(2)^3(1)^2}&=\frac{z_2}{(3)^3(-2)^2}\\[1mm] z_2&=189 \end{align*} $\thus$ When $x=3$ and $y=-2$, $z=189$.

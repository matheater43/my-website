Program Logic

If the coefficients of $Y$ involve decimals or fractions, a common multiple of the denominators is recorded as $D$. So, by replacing $Y$ by $DY$, the coefficients of $Y$ are all integers. \begin{align*} Y&=AX^3+BX^2+CX+M\\ &=A(10^4 i)^3+B(10^4 i)^2+C(10^4 i)+M\\ &=A(-10^{12}i)+B(-10^8)+C(10^4i)+M\\ &=(-B\times10^8+M)+(-A\times10^{12}+C\times10^4)i \end{align*} $A$, $B$, $C$ and $M$ are then extracted one by one by rounding off the real and imaginary parts to appropriate digits. After that, each coefficient is divided by $D$ to retrieve the coefficients of the original expression. Note that re-scaling the coefficients is not necessary to solve the equation.
To solve the cubic equation, consider \begin{align*} Ax^3+Bx^2+Cx+M&=0\\[1mm] x^3+\dfrac{B}{A}x^2+\dfrac{C}{A}x+\dfrac{M}{A}&=0\ \text{......(1)} \end{align*} We proceed by completing the cube which eliminates the $x^2$ term. Observe that
$(x+k)^3=x^3+3kx^2+3k^2x+k^3$ ......(2)
Comparing the coefficients of $x^2$ in (1) and (2), we have
\begin{align*} 3k&=\dfrac{B}{A}\\[1mm] k&=\dfrac{B}{3A} \end{align*} So (1) can be rewritten as \begin{align*} x^3+3\left(\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)x^2+3\left(\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)^2x+\left(\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)^3+\dfrac{C}{A}x+\dfrac{M}{A}-3\left(\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)^2x-\left(\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)^3&=0\\[1mm] \left(x+\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)^3+\left(\dfrac{C}{A}-\dfrac{B^2}{3A^2}\right)x+\dfrac{M}{A}-\dfrac{B^3}{27A^3}&=0 \end{align*}
By substituting $y=x+\dfrac{B}{3A}$, we have
\begin{align*} y^3+\left(\dfrac{C}{A}-\dfrac{B^2}{3A^2}\right)\left(y-\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)+\dfrac{M}{A}-\dfrac{B^3}{27A^3}&=0\\[1mm] y^3+\left(\dfrac{C}{A}-\dfrac{B^2}{3A^2}\right)y-\dfrac{BC}{3A^2}+\dfrac{B^3}{9A^3}+\dfrac{M}{A}-\dfrac{B^3}{27A^3}&=0\\[1mm] y^3+\left(\dfrac{C}{A}-\dfrac{B^2}{3A^2}\right)y+\dfrac{2B^3}{27A^3}-\dfrac{BC}{3A^2}+\dfrac{M}{A}&=0\\[1mm] y^3+3py+2q&=0\text{ ......(3)} \end{align*}
where $3p=\dfrac{C}{A}-\dfrac{B^2}{3A^2}\Leftrightarrow p=\dfrac{C}{3A}-\dfrac{B^2}{9A^2}$
and $2q=\dfrac{2B^3}{27A^3}-\dfrac{BC}{3A^2}+\dfrac{M}{A}\Leftrightarrow q=\dfrac{B^3}{27A^3}-\dfrac{BC}{6A^2}+\dfrac{M}{2A}$
Now suppose $y=u+v$. We have \begin{align*} y^3&=(u+v)^3\\ &=u^3+3u^2v+3uv^2+v^3\\ &=u^3+3uv(u+v)+v^3\\ &=u^3+3uvy+v^3\\ y^3-3uvy-(u^3+v^3)&=0\text{ ......(4)} \end{align*} Comparing (3) and (4), we have \begin{alignat*}{2} -3uv&=3p \quad &&\quad & -(u^3+v^3)&=2q\\ uv&=-p \quad &&\quad & u^3+v^3&=-2q\\ u^3v^3&=-p^3 \quad &&\quad & \end{alignat*} Hence, $u^3$ and $v^3$ are the roots of the quadratic equation $z^2+2qz-p^3=0$, which gives \begin{align*} u^3,\ v^3&=\dfrac{-2q\pm\sqrt{(2q)^2-4(-p)^3}}{2}\\[1mm] &=q\pm\sqrt{q^2+p^3} \end{align*} So the solutions for $u$ and $v$ are $\sqrt[3]{q\pm\sqrt{q^2+p^3}},\ w\sqrt[3]{q\pm\sqrt{q^2+p^3}},\ \overline{w}\sqrt[3]{q\pm\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}$
where $w=-\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{i\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is a complex 3rd root of unity.
Since $u$ and $v$ have to satisfy the condition that $uv = -p$ is a real number, the solutions are given by \begin{align*} x&=y-\dfrac{B}{3A}\\[1mm] &=u+v-\dfrac{B}{3A}\\[1mm] &=\sqrt[3]{q+\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}+\sqrt[3]{q-\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}-\dfrac{B}{3A}=:r\\[1mm] &\quad\text{or }w\sqrt[3]{q+\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}+\overline{w}\sqrt[3]{q-\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}-\dfrac{B}{3A}=:\alpha\\[1mm] &\quad\text{or }\overline{w}\sqrt[3]{q+\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}+w\sqrt[3]{q-\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}-\dfrac{B}{3A}=:\overline{\alpha} \end{align*} In the program, the line 
B³ - B Ans⌟2 − M⌟(2A → M :
is \begin{align*} \left(-\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)^3-\left(-\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)\left(\dfrac{C}{A}\right)\times\dfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{M}{2A}&=-\dfrac{B^3}{27A^2}+\dfrac{BC}{6A^2}-\dfrac{M}{2A}\\ &=-q \end{align*} The line 
√((C⌟3 − B²)³ + Ans²M+ :
is \begin{align*} -q+\sqrt{\left[\left(\dfrac{C}{A}\right)\times\dfrac{1}{3}-\left(-\dfrac{B}{3A}\right)^2\right]^3+(-q)^2}&=-q+\sqrt{\left(\dfrac{C}{3A}-\dfrac{B^2}{9A^2}\right)^3+q^2}\\[1mm] &=-q+\sqrt{q^2+p^3} \end{align*} This number is real when it is equal to its complex conjugate, in which case the real root $r$ defined above is computed.
If this number has a non-zero imaginary part, then we have to take its cube root as a complex number.
We write \begin{align*} M&=(re^{i\theta})^3\\ &=r^3e^{3i\theta} \end{align*} So the complex root is \begin{align*} \sqrt[3]{q+\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}+\sqrt[3]{q-\sqrt{q^2+p^3}}&=2\text{Re}(u)\\ &=2r\cos\theta\\ &=2\sqrt[3]{M}\cos\left(\dfrac{\arg M}{3}\right) \end{align*}
To find the other two roots, note that $-\dfrac{B}{A}=r+\alpha+\overline{\alpha}$ and $\dfrac{C}{A}=r\alpha+r\overline{\alpha}+\alpha\overline{\alpha}$.
So the line 
M⌟2 + √(AM − C + M²⌟4M-◢
is \begin{align*} \dfrac{\alpha+\overline{\alpha}}{2}+\sqrt{r(\alpha+\overline{\alpha})-(r\alpha+r\overline{\alpha}+\alpha\overline{\alpha})+\dfrac{(\alpha+\overline{\alpha})^2}{4}} &=\text{Re}(\alpha)+\sqrt{-\alpha\overline{\alpha}+\dfrac{\alpha^2+2\alpha\overline{\alpha}+\overline{\alpha}^2}{4}}\\[1mm] &=\text{Re}(\alpha)+\sqrt{\dfrac{\alpha^2-2\alpha\overline{\alpha}+\overline{\alpha}^2}{4}}\\[1mm] &=\text{Re}(\alpha)+\sqrt{\dfrac{(\alpha-\overline{\alpha})^2}{4}}\\[1mm] &=\text{Re}(\alpha)+\sqrt{\dfrac{[2\text{Im}(\alpha)i]^2}{4}}\\[1mm] &=\text{Re}(\alpha)+\sqrt{-\text{Im}(\alpha)^2}\\[1mm] &=\text{Re}(\alpha)\pm\text{Im}(\alpha)i \end{align*}
